# ADR-027 — Bounded diagnostic timeline and app-owned data lifecycle

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-10-29

**Decision owners:** Product owner, implementation reviewer and security/privacy reviewer

**Related requirements:** `FR-DIA-01–05`, `FR-HIS-01–06`, `FR-DAT-06–07/12–15`, `AC-DAT-06–07/12–17`, `NFR-REL-02`, `NFR-MNT-01–04`, `NFR-OBS-01–05`

**Related decisions:** D4; Q1/Q5/Q6/Q8; ADR-010/013/014/015/016/019/028/029; Proposed ADR-023

**Owner acceptance:** 2026-10-29 — the owner answered **“Yes accepted”** to ADR-027/028/029 including the final R1 deletion clarification, after Claude Opus 5.5 cleared F1–F13. Option A and the stated v1 policy/engineering defaults are accepted. The discussion history below records earlier recommendations/pending selections and is superseded by this final acceptance. Model/API/measurement/migration/implementation evidence remains gated; acceptance is not a capability or host-action claim. ADR-028/029 are also Accepted; ADR-023 remains Proposed.

## Context and authority

The specification §§9.16, 11.5 and 12.3–12.5 permits a consented, unelevated, read-only timeline with 30-day local snapshot retention, pre-persistence redaction and preserved OS records/exports/update history on scan clear. This accepted ADR settles the Task 46 collection/field/correlation/lifecycle design, including the stated engineering defaults. New operation-history/favourites lifecycle remains the separate Q5/ADR-023 gate. No host access or implemented capability is inferred from policy acceptance.

### Owner discussion history — superseded by final acceptance above

In response to the first Task 46 decision round, the owner selected **seven days** of diagnostic lookback, **yes to showing available evidence with explicit partial coverage when sources are inaccessible**, and a **15-minute proximity window**. These settle those three product choices only. The ADR as a whole remains Proposed; other count/byte/time/field/lifecycle limits and detailed correlation mechanics still await review. In particular, the seven-day answer does not independently accept the suggested 2,000-record/60-second collection limits. Existing 30-day snapshot retention remains unchanged. No implementation or host read is authorized by these selections.

In the second decision round the owner answered **“As recommended”** to questions 4–6. For this lifecycle ADR, that selects **Clear scan data also deletes saved AI explanations derived from the cleared data**, while preserving update history, the protected API key and spending records; deleting content never refunds recorded spending. Credential Manager and manual-retry/concurrency/deadline selections are recorded in ADR-028. Other lifecycle/backup/retention-cap mechanics and full ADR acceptance remain pending.

In response to question 10, **“10 & 12 can stay as recommend”** selects the presented diagnostic limits: **500 records per source, 2,000 overall, 8 MiB per collection/export, 64 MiB retained payload, and a 60-second collection deadline**, with visible truncation/omission and oldest inactive snapshot removal at capacity even when this shortens 30-day retention. Those values and capacity behavior are owner selections. Per-record/text/per-reader sublimits, exact source fields, detailed mechanics and whole-ADR acceptance remain pending; none is proven by selecting a policy.

After review F5, the owner agrees to the source-kind distinction: **Clear scan data deletes diagnostic-timeline explanations with the snapshots they explain, but preserves failed-update explanations with update history**. Failed-update explanations remain subject to their own normal 30-day/cap expiry and are deleted by the existing **Clear update history** control or Factory reset. Store required closed source provenance so this is deterministic; deleting either content kind never refunds accounting. This clarifies question 4 rather than authorizing deletion of all saved AI content on scan clear.

Current `BoundedScanEventSink` and `BoundedUpdateExecutionEventSink` hold bounded in-memory app events. `SqliteDatabase.GetTimelineAsync` reads app history; none proves OS-reader coverage. `FactoryReset` currently removes the exact schedule, downloads, logs and database artifacts and rejects unexpected owned-root objects. Its existing final/recovery paths do not prove removal of new external credential state. Task 46 changes no implementation, accepted ADR or reset design.

## Decision drivers and options considered

| Option | Benefits | Costs and risks |
|---|---|---|
| A — Manual bounded snapshots with typed fields and deterministic correlation (**recommended**) | Explicit consent, repeatable fixtures, small retained schema, visible partial coverage; no resident process. | Less context than full logs; caps can omit useful evidence; source access must be proven later. |
| B — Continuously ingest broad logs and infer relationships | More historical detail. | Unbounded/sensitive data, background activity and causal overclaiming conflict with the agreed scope. Rejected. |
| C — Show only PC Steward history | Reuses current storage and avoids OS-reader uncertainty. | Does not deliver accepted `FR-DIA-01`; remains a fallback view when sources are unavailable, not a complete diagnostic timeline. |

## Decision

Accept A. Application owns consent, bounded orchestration and lifecycle ports; Domain owns deterministic ordering/correlation; adapters own narrowly allowlisted OS reads and sanitization. SQLite remains persistence-owned under ADR-015. No reader changes OS state, invokes update providers, elevates or runs from ScheduledHost. Existing software scanning does not imply diagnostic consent. Use **per-collection consent**: each manual review names sources, fields, bounds and local retention; cancel changes nothing. No remembered collection grant or background collector is added. Source-selection preferences are not collection authority; export and AI egress remain separate approvals. The owner accepted this v1 consent/default policy with the whole ADR.

### Q1 — Accepted collection policy v1

| Setting | Accepted value and exact meaning |
|---|---|
| Lookback | **Owner selected: seven days.** Use seven elapsed days ending at the captured collection-start UTC instant; narrower user selection allowed. No automatic expansion after an empty result. |
| Sources | Collect PC Steward verified operation history first, then System Event Log, Application Event Log, Reliability and read-only Windows Update history. Five separately reported results. App history is first so OS saturation cannot remove all app changes needed for proximity labels; remaining OS count/time allowances are shared as below. |
| Record limits | **Owner selected: at most 500 per source and 2,000 overall.** Apply the time/provider/channel/event-kind allowlist in the source-side query before the count limit; the cap is on matching records returned/materialized for inspection, including matching records later rejected by validation, not the first 500 unrelated raw events. No unbounded client-side filtering. After app history returns `a <= 500` matching records, each of the four OS readers receives `min(500, floor((2000-a)/4))` matching-record slots, newest first; do not redistribute unused slots in v1. This is 375 each when app history fills 500, or 500 each if app history is empty. Global byte/time caps can still truncate and must be disclosed. |
| Byte limits | **Owner selected: 8 MiB per collection/export and 64 MiB total retained diagnostic payload.** Use at most 4 KiB serialized UTF-8 per projected record. Count envelope/source-status overhead too; reserve space for truncation/status metadata before accepting records. MiB means 1,048,576 bytes. |
| Text | No raw rendered messages/XML. One optional sanitized source-specific summary of at most 512 Unicode scalar values, still subject to the 4 KiB record limit. Source text never supplies a path, query or executable command. |
| Time and concurrency | **Owner selected: 60-second overall collection deadline.** Use one reader at a time, app history first with at most 15 seconds. For each remaining OS reader use at most `min(15 seconds, remaining overall time / OS readers still to start)`, so an early slow source does not deliberately consume every later reader's time. The overall monotonic deadline always wins, including orchestration/write overhead; timeout/partial remains visible. A route that cannot apply bounded matching query/enumeration/cancellation stays unsupported. |
| Retention | D4's 30 days remains accepted. Following F8, expire **whole immutable snapshots** 30 elapsed days from their captured collection instant, or earlier at the accepted capacity/clear/reset boundary. Viewing/exporting a snapshot cannot refresh it; a newly consented collection creates a distinct snapshot. Do not expire individual records out of a frozen snapshot. **Owner selected: at the aggregate cap, prune oldest inactive snapshots first and disclose that capacity can shorten retention.** Never claim 30 days of complete coverage. |
| Export | One user-requested UTF-8 JSON projection of a selected snapshot, with the same field/redaction rules and 8 MiB ceiling; no archive, raw-log attachment or automatic save. Preview exactly the projected records and source coverage, warn about sensitivity, then use an explicit destination. |

These are resource proposals, not performance evidence. F2 puts app history first; re-check R2 adds bounded equal OS count slots and remaining-time shares rather than filling sources serially to the global cap. Owner maximums are unchanged; underfilled sources may leave unused slots instead of adding redistribution complexity. R3 requires filtering at the source before matching-record admission, not a seven-day coverage claim from 500 arbitrary raw events. Missing/unsupported/limited sources remain explicit. Fixtures cover five saturated sources, empty app/source results, invalid matched records, many irrelevant raw events, time allocation, cancellation and global byte/count limits. Tasks 64–66 must measure responsiveness/resources under ADR-006; logical payload bounds do not bound SQLite/WAL/backups, which need separate measured limits. Existing ADR-016 log limits remain separate.

### Q1/Q6 — Minimum source schema and coverage

All fields are optional unless their absence makes identity/time unusable. Common fields are schema/policy version, app-local snapshot ID/collection UTC/expiry, closed source kind, source-local record identity, source occurrence UTC/known offset, closed event/outcome kind and sanitized code/summary. Snapshot collection/expiry fields are required for lifecycle enforcement; source occurrence time does not substitute for them. Do not store computer name, SID/user/account, serial, device identity, full paths, command lines, raw XML, exception objects, dumps or complete source objects. Identifiers are minimized source-local evidence, never execution authority. Outgoing projections omit internal IDs unless essential and separately approved.

| Source | Minimum projection and admission rule |
|---|---|
| System/Application | Closed provider/channel/event-ID/version mapping for unexpected shutdown, bugcheck and application fault/report records; source record ID, occurrence time, event kind and allowlisted numeric result code. Kernel-Power 41, EventLog 6008, BugCheck 1001 and WER/application-fault examples in the spec are not an event-ID-only filter. Exact provider/version mappings and projected fields require fixture/API verification before collection ships. |
| Reliability | Bounded source record identity/time, closed problem kind and sanitized application display name when needed; no report files, device/user details or arbitrary extra properties. |
| Windows Update history | Bounded update identity/time, operation/result enum, sanitized title and numeric result code; no update search, download, installation or repair action. |
| PC Steward history | App operation identity/time, provider/package/version, typed outcome/restart evidence and already sanitized failure category; only records actually present. Proposed install/uninstall history is not assumed implemented. |

Each source reports separately: available/empty, partial/truncated, denied, unsupported, timed out, cancelled or failed; include queried interval and a bounded typed reason. Missing/denied sources may produce an explicitly partial timeline (**owner selected: yes to partial results**). Zero accessible sources is “no diagnostic coverage”, never a clean-health result. Do not probe this owner's records to accept the design; exact host access checks require separate authority. Readers use only documented read-only routes whose scope and bounds are proven at implementation; no unrestricted WMI/registry fallback and never `Win32_Product`.

### Q6 — Accepted deterministic timeline rules

- Normalize known instants to UTC, preserve known original offset/precision, and format local time only for display. Resolve neither ambiguous local/DST time nor missing time by guessing: retain a visible untimed/ambiguous item, excluded from proximity labels. Receipt time is not event time. Future/conflicting times are flagged, not silently clamped.
- Sort by occurrence UTC, closed source order, then validated source-local identity; untimed items have a separate stable section. Duplicate repeated records within a source by its validated identity and channel/log generation where available. If a source lacks a reliable identity, deduplicate only identical normalized projected fields within that source/collection and mark identity weak. Do not deduplicate across sources or equate two faults solely by timestamp/text.
- **Owner selected: 15 minutes on either side of the incident.** Use a symmetric window be inclusive at both boundaries. Label each app change with a known operation-completion instant in that window “occurred near”; unknown/in-progress times remain visibly uncertain. No matching on receipt time, transitive chains, causal rankings or health scores. Kernel-Power 41 indicates an unexpected shutdown; its reporting timestamp is not proof of the actual shutdown instant or cause.
- Freeze policy version, source coverage and selected incident with the immutable snapshot so the same live snapshot/selection yields the same deterministic result. After whole-snapshot expiry/clear/pruning, report it unavailable rather than silently replaying fewer records; do not save submitted AI evidence to recreate it. Redaction and truncation remain visible. AI never changes deterministic correlation or eligibility.

### Shared lifecycle manifest — accepted additive contract

Store diagnostic snapshots, saved redacted explanations and minimal accounting as separate logical SQLite categories with no new persistent file root. This avoids accidental deletion through scan foreign-key cascades; saved explanations are self-contained sanitized content without their submitted prompt. **Require source kind** (`DiagnosticTimeline` or `FailedUpdate`) and a validated app-local origin ID (snapshot or update job/history identity) for each saved explanation. First-version requests have one typed source kind; unknown/mixed/tampered provenance cannot be saved as a valid item. Source provenance selects content-clearing behavior and never supplies execution, path or accounting-deletion authority. Existing update history stays preserved on scan clear. New install/uninstall history and favourites remain **Q5 pending** under ADR-023: provisionally follow preserved operation-history/local-preference categories, but do not assign diagnostic retention or create their schema here.

| Category | Expiry / Clear scan data | Clear downloads / logs / update history | Provider disconnect | Factory reset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collected snapshots and derived timeline indexes | 30 days/cap as above; scan clear removes both atomically. | **R1 accepted:** preserve the entire immutable snapshot, including captured minimal app-history copies, when live update history is cleared. Do not remove records/references from frozen replay. Disclose preserved copies in that clear confirmation. | Preserve. | Delete database category/artifacts. |
| Diagnostic-timeline explanation content (`DiagnosticTimeline`) | Normal 30-day/cap expiry; **owner selected: scan clear removes these explanations with diagnostic snapshots**, without touching accounting. | Download/log/update-history clearing preserves this content, subject to its own expiry. | Preserve until normal expiry, scan clear or reset; disclose. | Delete with all app-owned AI content. |
| Failed-update explanation content (`FailedUpdate`) | Normal 30-day/cap expiry; **owner selected: scan clear preserves these explanations with update history**. | Download/log clear preserves; **Clear update history deletes failed-update explanations with their history**, without touching accounting. | Preserve until normal expiry, update-history clear or reset; disclose. | Delete with all app-owned AI content. |
| Minimal spending/usage, unresolved reservations, accounting continuity | No 30-day content expiry; preserve on scan clear. ADR-029 governs compaction. | Preserve; a content/history clear never refunds spend. | Preserve; key rotation/reconnection never creates allowance. | Delete; AI re-enablement requires ADR-029's continuity gate. |
| Diagnostic/AI consent and settings | Preserve; consent revocation stops further collection/sends and invalidates previews. | Preserve. | Revoke AI consent and clear pending drafts; diagnostic consent separate. | Delete. |
| Protected provider key | Never in SQLite/config/log/export/backup; preserve on scan clear. | Preserve. | Exact compiled credential target deletion and absence verification, ADR-028. | Same exact external deletion; not just database removal. |
| Windows logs/Reliability/Update records, provider caches, installed apps, user exports | Preserve under every routine control and expiry. | Preserve. | Preserve. | Preserve. |

The owner clarified question 4 through review F5: scan clear removes diagnostic-derived explanation content, while update-history clear removes failed-update explanation content. These remain unimplemented extensions rather than existing `FR-DAT-07` behavior. Each operation removes its selected source/content rows transactionally and preserves independent accounting/keys and the other content kind. Existing Clear logs/Clear update history controls are documented in the accepted factory-reset design and present in Desktop; no new clear-all/history feature is added. Consent revocation is not a hidden clear operation. Explain deletes/preserves before confirmation. Tests must create both explanation kinds, exercise each clear/expiry/reset path, reject invalid provenance and prove no reservation/usage deletion or refund.

The owner explicitly accepted R1: **preserve frozen diagnostic snapshots intact** when Clear update history deletes live update-history rows and `FailedUpdate` explanations. Its confirmation must say that captured history copies and `DiagnosticTimeline` explanations remain; use Clear scan data as well to remove those, or they expire/prune/reset normally. Captured app-history records are snapshot-owned minimal sanitized copies, not live lookups or foreign keys that cascade-delete/block the snapshot. This preserves deterministic replay; do not silently remove records. The alternative of removing affected whole snapshots was not selected. Test the control, copy independence and replay/confirmation together. This choice was accepted with the final ADR set, not inferred from the earlier F5 answer.

**Accepted additive 2.0 amendment contract:** ADR-010/016 source schemas/redaction/export/no prompt persistence; ADR-015 logical categories/retention/migration/backup/non-cascading history; ADR-013/019 and `FACTORY_RESET_DESIGN.md` external credential/new logical categories; ADR-014 capability composition. This ADR governs those planned additions. Earlier ADR/reset documents still describe the existing implementation baseline and are not rewritten wholesale here; Task 47 briefs and the relevant implementation tasks must carry these accepted extensions and align their detailed design/evidence before the added capabilities ship. Specification v0.4 separately records the owner-authorized editable limits. No wildcard credential enumeration, root expansion or paths from records/settings are permitted.

Reset must retain ADR-013's double confirmation, fixed sentinel, exclusive cross-process data-operation lease and startup-before-store ordering. Extend every marker transition/recovery/onboarding fast path to verify the exact credential absent before success, including when the database is already absent; an old marker cannot stand as proof for a new category. Specify versioned marker compatibility before shipping. A missing credential is idempotent; denied/locked/unsafe/unknown state is a visible residual and blocks completion. No credential secret or external target from marker contents is accepted. Existing handle-relative non-following deletion applies to compiled file categories. Quiesce AI/collection operations first, cancel safely and record possible spend before reset; no OS-record deletion or network deletion call.

Migration/backup recovery must not resurrect expired or explicitly cleared diagnostic/AI content: owner-reviewed backup expiry/invalidation and restore filtering are prerequisites, including backup sidecars and retention copies under fixed authority. No plaintext or protected key enters an app backup. Logical deletion/expiry is not a forensic secure-erasure promise for SQLite/WAL, filesystem snapshots or user-managed external backups. Report completion/residuals accurately and document that boundary.

## Consequences and evidence gates

The result is small, explainable and testable but can be incomplete, and capacity pruning can shorten the 30-day content window. Collection cancellation or a failed write preserves the prior committed snapshot; deliberately accepted partial collection is stored with all source statuses. Scan clear is transactional across snapshots/indexes/explanation content and respects active work. Retention is bounded opportunistic maintenance at start/use, not a new scheduled/resident collector; expired content is excluded before display/export/send even if cleanup fails, with residuals reported.

Tasks 64–66 must cover exact source allowlists/fields, denied/empty/all-denied inputs, enumeration/count/bytes/time bounds, hostile text, sanitization before every sink, fixed-clock/DST/time-zone/conflicting-time fixtures, ties/duplicates/correlation boundaries, unrelated nearby changes and no causal Kernel-Power claim. Add migration/backup resurrection, whole-snapshot expiry/immutable replay, source-specific explanation clear behavior, active collection/clear/reset races, atomic cancellation, locked files/credential failures, old marker/recovery paths, idempotence and preserved OS/export/history fixtures. Use synthetic records and approved isolated roots; approved later host access evidence proves coverage only for that host. No VM or destructive host test is authorized by this proposal.

## Reconsider when

Owner changes accepted scope/limits, source APIs cannot enforce bounded unelevated reads, measurements cannot meet NFR budgets, necessary fields defeat minimization, or lifecycle/backup/reset proofs fail. Keep the affected reader or new feature blocked until a narrower accepted design is viable.
